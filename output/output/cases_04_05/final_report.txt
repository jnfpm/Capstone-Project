CLEAR - DOCUMENTED PAYMENT AND EMPTY EVIDENCE
Development evaluation, 15 November 2026

1. WHAT THIS SESSION IS ABOUT

Case 04 asks whether prosecution and defense can use a supplied agreement and
invoice to explain a payment without treating those documents as proof of
legitimacy. Case 05 asks whether the software refuses to investigate a case
with no evidence before asking a language model for an answer.

These are different checks. Case 04 produces two live reports. Case 05 should
produce no report and make no model calls.

The data are fictional. The transaction is 1,200 USD, from bank-A:customer-04
to bank-B:provider-04, on 8 September 2022 at 09:00. Both document excerpts
describe an August bookkeeping fee with those accounts, amount and due date.
The agreement mentions a monthly fee. Only ONE actual payment is supplied.
The simulated score is 0.78 and the alert is true; there are no model reasons.

2. WHAT WAS ALREADY IMPLEMENTED

3. THE EXPERIMENTS, IN ORDER

A. baseline_20261115: connection failure, NOT GRADED.
Both role attempts failed with APIConnectionError in the restricted environment.
There were no reports to evaluate. The failure was saved, not hidden or counted
as evidence that the prompts were bad.

B. baseline_retry_20261115: original prompts, real reports.
The same baseline was run with network access outside the restricted environment.
Prosecution returned two findings and defense three.

Both reviewers used the documents and supported the main comparison. However,
prosecution said: "Further investigation would be warranted only if those
documents or the underlying relationship cannot be verified."
The packet cannot establish that exclusive rule. Verification is useful, but
successful verification would not rule out every other possible concern.
Defense described the transfer and then described its match to the documents,
repeating information across its first two findings.
Verdict: baseline needs revision, especially the prosecution conclusion.

C. documents_v2_20261115: general document rules added.
Both roles returned two findings. The document-to-payment comparison was more
compact. The new rules distinguished stated purpose from verified authenticity,
service delivery and a proven history of recurring payments.

Prosecution still wrote: "The only grounds for further review are ..." and
included unverified documents among those grounds. That phrasing remained too
exclusive and blurred missing information with evidence supporting suspicion.
Verdict: improvement, but not yet accepted as the final pair.

D. documents_v3_20261115: information gaps are not suspicious evidence.
The exclusive investigation rule disappeared. Both roles returned two findings.
However, prosecution's assessment said "The matching terms do not verify ..."
while citing only the two documents and model outputs. That sentence relies on
the match with the transaction, whose supporting fields were cited only in the
previous finding. Those citations do not carry over automatically.
Verdict: the reasoning improved, but the final assessment still needed its own
complete support for the comparison.

E. documents_v4_20261115: comparisons need both sides in EACH finding.
The instructions now say to cite both document and transaction facts wherever
an assessment repeats the match, or to state the verification limitation without
repeating that comparison. Both roles returned two findings.

The defense assessment cites both documents plus amount, currency, sender,
recipient and timestamp fields. The prosecution assessment states the document
verification limits directly and cites the model and document facts, with the
account fields for the accounts it mentions. There is no exclusive rule about
closing or continuing an investigation.
Verdict: acceptable development result. Minor verbosity remains, especially
some repeated account names and a short verification qualification in the
prosecution observation. This is not a claim of perfect prose.

F. documents_v4_check_20261115: repeat without changing the prompts.
Both reports again have two findings. The first compares the payment with the
documents. The second gives the role's interpretation, model limitations and
records needed. The defense assessment again includes both sides of its match.
Prosecution avoids asserting a new investigation rule. Neither reviewer claims
innocence, authenticated documents, verified services or observed monthly history.
Verdict: acceptable repeat on this development case. This is a consistency check,
not an independent evaluation dataset.

4. HOW THE FINAL CASE 04 REPORTS WERE ASSESSED

Each candidate and confirmation run was read finding by finding against its
saved evidence. The final case 04 candidates are v6, not the earlier v4/v5 runs:

- Amount and currency: USD is supported by amount_usd, or by Amount Paid plus
  Payment Currency. The actual and documented amounts agree.
- Account pair: both transaction endpoints support the comparison; the two
  documents identify the same payer and recipient.
- Date: the transaction timestamp supports payment on the documented due date.
- Purpose: the bookkeeping explanation is attributed to the agreement/invoice,
  not invented from a payment format, country or risk score.
- Interpretation: a plausible explanation is not proof of authenticity,
  ownership, delivery of services or innocence.
- Recurrence: a contractual monthly fee is not an observed monthly history.
- Model output: the supplied alert and score are review signals, not a verified
  probability of crime. Missing reasons are acknowledged.
- Repetition: one principal comparison plus one assessment replaces the initial
  defense report's separate transfer summary and document comparison.

The record count is a property of the supplied packet, not a claim that no other
payments exist. Some absence statements are packet-level limitations; an evidence
ID does not prove that the corresponding information is absent from the world.

6. ORIGINAL CASES: REGRESSION REVIEW

The v4 confirmation also reran cases 01, 02 and 03. Cases 02 and 03 retained
adequate results. In case 01, the assessment referred to several sources or
counterparties but cited their common destination rather than their source
account fields. The observation had those fields; the assessment did not.
That was a citation gap worth correcting, even though the underlying statement
was true in the full packet. The earlier v10_opus_check reports did include
those source references. One rerun cannot establish that the new document rules
caused the gap; generative variability is also possible.

G. final_v5_20261115: a source-diversity citation rule.
The final change requires source-account references for claims about distinct
senders, and recipient-account references for distinct recipients, in both
drafting and editing. This is a general rule, not a hard-coded case answer.
All four live cases are rerun with these same final prompts.

Cases 01-03 passed the qualitative development review in v5:
- Case 01: both assessments now cite the incoming source accounts. Totals
  (15,000 USD), outgoing amount (14,900 USD), difference (100 USD) and elapsed
  times (15 and 5 minutes) are correct and supported. The difference is not
  presented as a final balance or proof of which funds financed the transfer.
- Case 02: both reports retain the 3,600 USD total, same account pair and
  31-day intervals. The scheduled-payment explanation stays hypothetical;
  no business purpose or contract is invented.
- Case 03: one finding per role acknowledges the missing transactions and
  requests the required information without inventing a transaction pattern.

The v5 case 04 prosecution was satisfactory. Its defense, however, hypothesized
that the transfer was the documented fee while citing the documents and only
the transaction timestamp, omitting amount and account support in that finding.
Removing explicit comparison words had not removed the inference's need for
support. I did not accept that defense report as final.

H. documents_v6_20261115: support the documented-payment hypothesis itself.
The document-specific instructions now require amount/currency, both accounts
and timestamp references for a hypothesis identifying a transfer as the payment
described in documents, even without the word "match". A timestamp alone does
not support that connection. The evidence and substantive criteria are unchanged.

Both v6 reports passed the development review, so v6 was repeated without
changing prompts (documents_v6_check_20261115). Both confirmation reports also
passed. In both defense assessments, the documented-payment hypothesis now
cites both documents, payment amount/currency, sender, recipient and timestamp.
Both prosecution reports avoid unsupported exclusive investigation rules.
No final report treats document authenticity, ownership, service delivery or
innocence as established. Each report has two findings, with no separate,
repeated transfer-summary finding.

I stopped changing the prompts at this point: the identified material failures
were corrected and a repeat supported consistency for case 04. This is not a
guarantee of perfect future output. The final v6 change is document-specific;
cases 01-03 were live-checked with v5, not rerun with v6. All 32 offline tests
passed again with v6. Remaining writing issues are minor verbosity and occasional
technical labels, rather than a material unsupported conclusion in these runs.

9. WHAT TO DO NEXT

Read the final case 04 confirmation alongside its document and transaction
evidence. Check one finding at a time, including the final hypothesis. Keep
these cases as a development set. Next, use new documented-payment examples
(including conflicting terms) and independent human assessment, instead of
continuing to tune the same example indefinitely. Separately complete the real
preprocessing handoff and account-history coverage before interpreting real
alerts. Debate remains a later task; it was not part of this experiment.

This is qualitative assessment by the coding assistant that also revised the
prompts. It is not independent human evaluation. Repeated use of this case makes
it a development case; successful reports do not establish general accuracy.
The semantic criteria were applied manually; passing JSON/ID checks alone does
not show that a claim is supported. More unseen cases and human review remain
necessary. Real-data integration and account-history retrieval were not tested
in these fictional-case experiments.
